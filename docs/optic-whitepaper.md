## 1. Digital life now produces a second self.

Today, a person's reputation is shaped by scattered signals: emails, calendar patterns, public profiles, search results, documents, metadata, social history, and institutional traces.

These signals influence how people are perceived by employers, investors, institutions, partners, and peers, but individuals rarely have a clear way to understand what their own data is signaling.

We live inside digital systems that constantly interpret us, yet we have almost no tools to inspect or improve those interpretations.

## 2. Optic is the private feedback layer.

Optic gives users a private way to understand how they may be perceived across personal, professional, and social contexts.

The product analyzes user-authorized data and turns it into a clear, actionable profile: what signals exist, what they may imply, where the risks or contradictions are, and how the user can improve the way they are understood.

The goal is not to punish users, expose them, or create a public ranking system. The goal is to give people the feedback loop that modern digital life has removed.

## 3. The first version

The first version of Optic is a local, opt-in audit tool.

Users connect a limited set of personal data sources, such as email, calendar metadata, documents, public profiles, and local files.

Optic processes this information privately on-device and produces an initial baseline: a structured view of the user's digital footprint, the patterns it reveals, and the specific areas where clarification, context, or improvement may be useful.

This gives users immediate value without requiring them to surrender control of their data.

## 4. Continuous calibration

Over time, Optic can become a continuous calibration layer.

Users may choose to contribute anonymized or privacy-preserving data to improve the system and, in return, participate in a user-owned data economy.

Instead of platforms extracting behavioral data without meaningful compensation, Optic creates a model where users understand the value of their data, control what is shared, and can benefit from contributing to the network.

## 5. Personal AI feedback

The long-term vision is a personal AI feedback system that helps people navigate an increasingly complex social and professional world.

As AI systems become more involved in hiring, fundraising, lending, reputation, dating, education, and daily communication, people will need their own tools to understand how they are being read.

Optic becomes that layer: a private, user-controlled system for self-understanding, reputation context, and social calibration.

## 6. What Optic is not

Optic is not a surveillance product. It is not a government social credit system. It is not a public score for institutions to use against people.

It is a user-side product that helps individuals see themselves more clearly, understand how their behavior and data may be interpreted, and make better decisions about how they present, communicate, and operate.

## 7. The starting wedge

We are starting with a narrow, useful wedge: private audits for people in high-stakes moments, such as founders preparing to fundraise, professionals preparing for hiring, creators managing public reputation, and individuals who want to understand their digital footprint.

From there, Optic can expand into a broader personal intelligence layer for identity, reputation, and behavioral feedback.

## 8. The core thesis

Modern life produces more signals than humans can interpret.

Optic turns those signals into private, actionable feedback.

It gives people a way to understand how they are seen, correct what is wrong, and regain agency over their digital identity.
