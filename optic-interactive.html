The Problem

Every high-stakes threshold — visa screening, background checks, recruiting filters, loan applications — is now gated by automated crawlers. These systems ingest fragments of your digital trace, guess who you are from pattern-matched priors, and generate reviews behind closed doors.1 You never see the report, you cannot correct name collisions, and when a machine misinterprets an employment gap or hallucinates a record, you bear the entire cost of the error.2

Optic is how you answer back.

01
Visa refusal rates for applicants from Nigeria and India routinely exceed 40% for Schengen and US visas. Automated prescreening plays a growing, largely undisclosed role — and the applicant is never told which signal decided them.
02
Walters v. OpenAI (2023). The mixed ruling exposed a structural gap: defamation law was never written for machine-generated hallucination. The person described has no clear right to correction.
A private scan, not a public score

Optic runs locally on your machine to reconstruct the public trail that automated screening portals see when they search your name.

Everything runs on your device. The scan makes no uploads and no outside calls — when you close the app, nothing leaves unless you choose to export it. You run it to see how you're read, find what's misread, and check it against your own documents.

The model doing the reading is called Iris. It works out how each kind of reviewer is likely to read you, marks the gaps and mismatches, and grades every claim by the evidence behind it. Iris is interpretive, not authoritative — it surfaces likely perspectives to help you prepare, not a verdict on anyone.

The shift from deletion to proof

Two mechanical changes have made passive reputation management obsolete.

01

LLM searches are replacing traditional indexing. Recruiter bots, background screening engines, and compliance portals now ask LLMs to summarize candidates. Because these systems compile text from old blogs, name collisions, and crawled snippets, they generate authoritative-sounding summaries with zero verification. You cannot appeal to a human clerk when a pre-screening database has already flagged your name.

02

Invisibility triggers automated flags. Traditional privacy tools charge $120/year to scrub your details from public brokers. But blank spaces are no longer safe. When a visa prescreener or hiring platform sees an empty profile, its algorithm infers high risk or low activity, filling the gap with pattern-matched risk flags. You cannot hide from crawlers anymore; you have to out-prove them.5

Deletion was the defense for an era of human reviewers. Proof is the only viable response to automated inference.

05
Services like DeleteMe and Kanary charge $100–200/year and together serve hundreds of thousands of subscribers. They sell invisibility — but inference engines don't respect blank spaces. They fill gaps with pattern-matched guesses that can be worse than the original data.

Data Ingestion Rules

We do not crawl public profiles or scrape third-party databases. Optic operates only on archives you have a legal right to export under GDPR and CCPA regulations.4 You can download what the major platforms hold on you, and Optic unifies these raw formats on-device, running compact models (Llama-3/Phi-3 via WebGPU) to parse your history against the signals agencies like Sterling and HireRight are known to weight — mismatched employment dates, unverified credentials, name collisions, compliance gaps. Built on evidence, not spin

Optic isn't reputation management. It doesn't bury links or game rankings — it reads your own record and grades each claim by how well it can be proven. Primary documents beat inferred patterns; an unverified guess stays marked as a guess. That grading is what makes the output something a third party can actually rely on.

A shareable, signed correction packet you can attach to a job application, a visa filing, or a KYC review — a portable PDF that carries your verified claims without exposing the files behind them. Cheaper for a screener to accept than to re-check, and harder to fake than a plain document.

Straight answers

The questions that come up most, answered without spin.

When the Stage 2 packet ships, why would a visa officer or recruiter trust one I made myself?+
Because the packet won't ask them to trust you — it will let them check. Optic signs every packet, and each claim links to a cryptographic hash of the document behind it. A screener can confirm the packet wasn't altered after signing and that the evidence is real, without ever opening your files. It's the difference between take my word for it and here's a receipt you can verify. The Stage 1 product live today is the read itself — the audit and rating; the shareable packet is the next stage.
Will employers and visa offices accept Optic packets?+
The packet is built as a standard PDF with embedded JSON-LD: a person can read it, and an applicant tracking system can parse it, with nothing special installed on their end. Getting screeners to actively prefer an Optic-verified packet is a go-to-market job, not a technical one — and it's exactly what the Stage 2 push is built to test. We're not claiming an integration that doesn't exist. We're building a format anyone can already open.
How is this different from reputation management or SEO?+
Reputation tools move sentiment — bury a bad link, push a flattering one, game a ranking. Optic touches none of that. It corrects facts against your own primary documents and grades every claim by how well it can be proven. An opinion never earns the grade of a proof, and money never moves a claim. We fix what's wrong; we don't decorate what's true.
What if Optic can't verify a claim, or I don't have the document?+
Then it stays marked unverified. Optic won't manufacture evidence or promote a guess into a fact to make your packet look stronger — that would defeat the whole point. An honest unproven is worth more to you than a fabricated certainty that collapses under the first follow-up question.
Is it legal to pull all this data about myself?+
It's already your right. GDPR Article 20, the CCPA, and Brazil's LGPD each guarantee a portable copy of the data a platform holds on you — that's what Google Takeout and the LinkedIn export exist to satisfy. Optic only ever works on archives you've chosen to download. It never scrapes strangers, buys broker data, or runs checks on people who didn't opt in.

A machine is describing you right now, to someone who will decide something about your life, and you are not in the room. Optic is how you get in.
